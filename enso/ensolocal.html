    What these numbers are. For each month, how much a year with this winter’s expected El Niño or La Niña strength has differed from an average year at this spot, 1979–2025: a straight-line fit of each past year’s value against that year’s strength (NOAA’s December–February Relative Oceanic Niño Index, RONI), read at the strength NOAA now expects. They are the same numbers as the maps, for the map box that contains your location. They describe what tends to happen, not a weather forecast: any single winter can differ a lot. Pale numbers in plain type could easily be chance (p ≥ 0.05 for that box’s fit).

    Data. Temperature, rain and snow: ERA5 reanalysis (ECMWF), 2° boxes (about 200 km). Tropical storms: NOAA IBTrACS, days with a storm in the 5° box (about 550 km). U.S. tornadoes, hail and thunderstorm wind: days with a report in the 2° box, NOAA NCEI Storm Events. The long-term warming trend is removed first. For the official outlook, see NOAA’s seasonal outlooks and ENSO discussion. Place search by Open-Meteo (what you type is sent there).
